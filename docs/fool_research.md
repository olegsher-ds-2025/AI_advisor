# Motley Fool Stock Advisor: what the data says

Generated by `python -m fool.report` from `advisor/fool/recs.parquet` (707 rows) and `prices.parquet`. Nothing here is a trading signal; it describes what the picks looked like and how they did.

## 1. Headline findings

- **Cadence is mechanical**: 95% of months have exactly 2 BUYs, 98% published Thursday/Friday, David and Tom alternate. ~24 BUYs a year over 2002-03..2025-04.
- **368 distinct companies**, 201 repeat BUYs; median market cap at rec $8.0B (mid/large cap, not micro-cap).
- **Typical pick is mediocre, the average is carried by a few giants.** 365-day median return 12.4%, mean 19.7%; only 47% of picks beat SPY over 365d (median excess -3.0%). Dropping the top 1% of picks moves mean 365d excess from +7.2% to +3.6%.
- **Survivorship**: 70 of 569 BUYs still have no recomputed 365d return (horizon not elapsed or no price history); results above describe the labelled subset only.

## 8. Why does Fool recommend these? What the data can and cannot say

**The dataset has no rationale text**: no thesis, no scores, no reason codes, only date, ticker, action and (for 2002-2020) returns. The proxies below are measured from prices at entry, so they are point-in-time.

Stated philosophy (general knowledge, not in this data): Stock Advisor targets holding 5+ years, favouring durable-growth, founder-led, category-leading companies; David's picks skew to disruptive innovators and Tom's to quality compounders. The data is consistent with a growth tilt (tech, consumer, communication) but cannot confirm intent.

## 9. Data quality and limits

- Survivorship: price history is missing for acquired/bankrupt names unless filled from Tiingo; remaining gaps listed in the pipeline log.
- 2020-2025 picks lack Fool's own returns; labels there come from our price recomputation only.
- Market cap and risk rating only exist in the xlsx era; sector is today's classification.
- Fool returns vs ours: correlation 0.98 on overlapping picks but entry-price conventions differ; use `ret_*`/`excess_*` for labels.
- 365d overlapping windows: consecutive picks share market regimes, so observations are not independent; use time-based splits.
- Forward-return survivors in recent years are right-censored: 2025 picks have fewer labelled horizons.

## 10. Next steps for the ML dataset

1. Join point-in-time fundamentals (`financials` by `filed` date) for picks in the lake; extend lake coverage to the 368 picked names.
2. Features at rec date: momentum, drawdown, volatility, valuation, growth, margins, sector, cap bucket, analyst, repeat flag.
3. Targets: `excess_365d > 0` (classification) and `excess_365d` (regression); evaluate with walk-forward by recommendation year.
4. Add a control group: non-picked universe stocks with matching dates, otherwise a model only learns 'what a stock looks like' rather than 'what Fool chooses'.
5. Text rationale: fool.com articles per pick (paywalled) would be the only way to model the 'why'.
