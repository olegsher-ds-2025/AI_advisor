How the IIX Confirms Market Pressure
The core logic of the IIX assumes that institutional investors dominate the market near the market close. If a stock closes near its high on high volume, institutions are aggressively buying. If it closes near its low on high volume, they are selling.
                          (2 * Close) - High - Low
IIX Raw Bar Value =  ----------------------------------  * Volume
                                 High - Low
Traders typically track a 21-period cumulative moving total or average of this raw value to smooth out random daily noise and identify sustained trends.
🔍 Key Confirmation Strategies
1. Trend and Breakout Confirmation
• Bullish Confirmation: When price breaks above a key resistance level and the IIX rises sharply into positive territory, it confirms the move is backed by true buying pressure. This validates the breakout and lowers the probability of a "bull trap."
• Bearish Confirmation: When price breaks below a support level and the IIX drops sharply into negative territory, it confirms genuine institutional selling pressure rather than a temporary low-liquidity slip.
2. Spotting Divergences (Advanced Warnings)
Divergences between price action and the IIX are highly reliable signals that a trend is running out of steam.
• Bearish Divergence (Selling Pressure Build-up): The price continues to make higher highs, but the IIX creates lower highs. This indicates that while the price looks bullish, the underlying institutional volume is quietly shifting toward distribution (selling).
• Bullish Divergence (Buying Pressure Build-up): The price continues to make lower lows, but the IIX forms higher lows. This shows that despite falling prices, heavy volume is accumulating near the daily lows—signaling institutional buying absorption.
⚖️ IIX vs. Chaikin Money Flow (CMF) & Accumulation/Distribution
While similar to other volume oscillators, the IIX features a distinct mathematical focus:
Indicator	Primary Formula Driver	Core Strength
Intraday Intensity Index (IIX)	Compares Close to the High-Low Range	Best at tracking institutional end-of-day actions.
Accumulation/Distribution (A/D)	Similar to IIX but scales differently	Focuses on cumulative continuous volume flow.
Chaikin Money Flow (CMF)	Uses A/D values divided by total volume over a fixed window (usually 21 days)	Oscillates cleanly around a zero line (+1 to -1) for fast momentum matching.
🛠️ Best Practices for Trading
• Pair with an EMA: Combine the IIX with a 50-period or 200-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) to ensure you are only taking confirmation signals aligned with the macro trend.
• Watch the Zero-Line Crossings: Treat the zero-line on the IIX indicator as the ultimate line of equilibrium. When the indicator hovers near zero, it signifies a balanced battle between buyers and sellers, often warning you to step aside during a choppy, sideways market.
